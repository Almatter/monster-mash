# Horde’s Judgment: isolated arena prototype

Open `test/crucible.html?seed=17`. This standalone page does not import the live Stage 4 engine, read/write profiles, award scores, change release gates, or replace the published Stage 4 map. The build change only adds version stamping for its new page. No new art is required: the prototype reuses generated champion selection cutouts, infantry/arbalists, guardian art, crystal shrines and spell textures. Pose bobbing and signature powers are simplified; this is not a champion balance or full native-kit test.

## Battlefield and play

The nominal arena is 3600 × 3200 world units. Eight seeded camps sit on a 1,140-unit circle around the central arena. Adjacent camps are about 873 units apart, leaving their 360-unit guardian perimeters separate. The default camera follows the controlled champion, or the selected spectator subject, at a closer uniform scale. Full map (M) toggles a live overview of all camps; closing it returns to the followed champion. Overview ground projection adapts to the screen aspect ratio while upright models retain their proportions. The overview does not pause combat or grant safety. There are no timed pickups or racing objectives. The floor is an intentional layout blockout.

Eight AI champions begin with 16 followers each. Reinforcements replenish armies every 3.5 seconds to the selected equal cap (12, 20 or 28 per army). The renderer/simulation support up to 224 active ordinary troops, plus eight champions, sixteen guardians and eight crystals. Ranged and melee troops collide and fight; champions clear groups and duel one another. Champions can attack while advancing through weak troops, raid weakened crystal positions, retreat to recover, and return to fight. The lane graph has one shared spoke per camp and a central ring road. Minions follow the home spoke out, traverse the central road, then take the target camp’s same spoke in reverse. Route waypoints are computed on that graph, including when an army retargets after a capture. Outside camp circles and the central arena, movement is clamped to the visible 120-unit-wide lanes. The renderer draws the exact same graph. Army targets are matched uniquely using distances from home crystals, rather than compressed central rally positions. This prevents north/south camps attracting most armies and makes the reported seed start with distinct adjacent targets. Orders stay stable while the target lives; after the first neighboring base falls, the other surviving home neighbor is preferred. Reservations may rebalance when a base falls so surviving armies do not duplicate a target or wait indefinitely for a claimed camp. One shared guardian target is held until it dies, followed by the remaining guardian and crystal; reinforcements inherit this order. AI champions escort their frontmost wave by remaining lane distance, clearing the route before committing to a defended camp. Escort plans refresh about three times per second. They move into the inner circle to fight an exposed crystal, rather than repeatedly firing into its ward.

Minions only acquire non-siege targets at adjacency: the two collision radii plus 16 world units (42 for ordinary troop pairs, 56 against champions). This applies to melee and ranged troops equally. They stop to attack from their current position and do not chase; once that enemy leaves adjacency, they resume the designated siege route immediately. Damage from a distant champion cannot override this adjacency rule. Actual damage sources are carried through projectiles and abilities. Inspect lists each army's target. Retreat uses separate enter/exit health thresholds to avoid endless low-health indecision.

Each 6,500 HP life crystal has two anchored 2,600 HP ranged guardians and three rearming mines. Both guardians cover enemies within 360 units of the crystal, just beyond the inner decorative camp circle. Turrets cannot acquire stationary guardians/crystals or enemies standing within their own home-base circle. Their projectiles disengage at the same perimeter, preventing homing shots from following enemies into another base. Champion attacks from outside that response ring cannot damage a guardian, including AoE overlaps; a generated ward effect signals the block. The dashed outer camp ring shows the firing perimeter. They normally target the closest enemy, but any in-range attacker damaging their champion immediately becomes the retaliation target for four seconds, refreshed or replaced by subsequent hits. This applies to champions and minions and survives ordinary perception updates.

Guardian hits against champions use the defensive multiplier of 1.8 and a 20% maximum-health per-hit limit; champion duels retain their 7.5% limit. Ordinary troops absorb guardian shots with a 0.2 damage multiplier, so a wave can screen the champion. Guardian incoming damage from an unsupported champion is multiplied by 0.10; a wave of at least three minions within the defense area opens a six-second siege window, using 0.75 instead. Minion attacks against guardians use 0.5. These rules apply equally to every camp. Offensive basic attacks or powers end respawn invulnerability. Crystals independently fire 380-damage defensive bolts at enemies inside the 190-unit inner base circle, even after both guardians die. They share retaliation priority when the attacker is inside that circle. Crystal shots use the same defensive damage scaling as guardians; champions outside the inner circle cannot snipe the crystal. Living guardians reduce incoming crystal damage by 82%; defeating them exposes the crystal. Guardian deaths persist. Champions return at full health after ten seconds while their crystal remains, with three seconds of arrival protection. Crystal destruction stops reinforcements. A champion without a crystal is eliminated on death; destroying a crystal during its owner's recall also eliminates that owner. The last surviving claimant wins. There is no automatic crystal decay or forced scripted winner.

**Watch all eight** lets every champion act autonomously. Choose a champion to take control; WASD/arrows move, Q uses its prototype power, Space uses its prototype ultimate, and basics auto-target. Touch provides a joystick and power buttons. Inspect opens crystal health, guardian counts, champion status, events, troop caps, speed controls and seeds. New battle randomizes the seed. Replay / use seed restarts the entered seed; the URL retains it for comparison. Control mode returns speed to 1× when selected. Prototype choices are not saved to the game's profile.

## Performance and checks

Simulation uses a fixed 30 Hz step, pooled troop slots, a spatial grid, staggered perception, bounded projectiles (96, with 16 slots reserved for guardian fire) and effects (72). The static arena is rasterized at half resolution into an 1800 × 1600 cache once per resize/reset; camera movement transforms that cache without rebuilding it each frame. Character models and spell textures are downsampled once on load; unit recolors are cached. Device pixel ratio is capped at 1.5. Rendering uses the display's frame cadence independently of simulation speed, and background-tab time is discarded on return.

All 514 automated project tests passed, including thirty-one prototype tests for wards, respawn/permanent death, bounded pools, powers, deterministic outcomes, movement, isolation, guardian burst resistance and supported versus solo raids. Production build passed. Browser tests cover default close view, overview visibility, live simulation while viewing the map, return to close view, seed replay, pause, keyboard/touch movement, powers and responsive layout with no page errors. With 224 troops, desktop frame median / 95th percentile was 10 / 10.1 ms; the 844 × 390 touch viewport at DPR 2 (render cap 1.5), with fourfold CPU throttling, measured 10 / 20 ms. These are development-machine observations, not measured phone hardware performance.

Lane regression cases verify incoming/outgoing spoke symmetry for every camp, graph-connected routes, returning down a captured camp’s lane when retargeting, lane width containment, expanded camp separation, long-range ward blocks, independent crystal attacks, and a complete supported capture of both guardians and the armed crystal. Other regression cases reproduce the reported seed's Hag/Devourer crossing problem, distinct neighboring reservations, the opposite-neighbor order after a capture, side-fight avoidance, frontal/contact engagement, both guardians firing beyond the camp circle, unsupported versus supported damage, and retaliation priority for all attacker kinds. Seeded match durations are simulation observations, not human win-rate estimates.

Reproduce with `node --test tests/crucible.test.mjs` and `node tests/crucible-browser.mjs`. Screenshots under `test-results/crucible-*` show the initial layout, clash and manual controls on desktop/touch.
