# The last claim

When two claimants remain, Stage 4 heralds the final duel with a single 2.8-second low horn challenge and accelerating drum roll. The D-minor war march at 128 BPM gives way to a separate F-sharp harmonic-minor score at 156 BPM, with brass phrases, rapid string-like ostinatos and double-time percussion. The background dips during the entrance so the change is audible. The herald and score use the music bus, honor music volume, mute and pause, and do not retrigger on repeated final-state updates. Seven looping stems replace the previous seven; no growing cache or permanent extra voices. There is no text announcement, map reveal or combat rule change. A result switches to the quieter results score; a defeated player instead hears their own champion's theme in the new drumless defeat mix, plus a short lament in that character's tonal center. Existing mute and interface/music volumes apply.

Every champion has a distinct acceptance of the mantle, speech and description of their fall. The player's selected name and title introduce the ending. Victory establishes them as the undisputed Lord of the Horde, leading the winter invasion of the Second Continent. An early elimination does not invent a winner; the paragraph names the victor if the player stays until the match resolves. “Will you rise again when it calls?” leads into the Rise again button.

Hell victories alone play a brief ascension using the existing generated idle, attack and ultimate artwork. It uses the full-resolution, exactly recolored atlas and power effect already loaded for the player, then dissolves into their 512px portrait. It is a live canvas presentation, not a newly drawn animation or a downloaded GIF. Reduced motion disables automatic playback, but the Hell Ascension button remains visible and explicit Play enables one viewing. Missing artwork shows a loading/retry state. Champions with a saved Hell victory can also open the animation from their mastery entry in Monster Records, using their current palette. Score-card image downloads remain static.

Champion deaths keep a rolling eight-second damage history, grouped into quarter-second source/type buckets. Records contain actual health and ward losses after mitigation, the final damage source and effects at death. The history has at most 160 groups per champion and 64 completed death reports. Reports are expandable in the existing battle events and the result card; no extra combat popup is introduced. They describe past damage and do not disclose a rival's unscouted build. Healing within the window may make cumulative losses exceed maximum health.
